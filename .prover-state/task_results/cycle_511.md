## Approach

Started with the requested sorry-first scaffold after the cycle 510
`padRight` block and verified `OpenMath/ButcherGroup.lean` compiled with
the scaffold. The initial planner-suggested shape
`∃ n, s + n = u + n` was not suitable for a cross-stage relation because
right cancellation makes it same-stage only. I first tried the allowed
`HEq` common-padding shape with separate padding counts, but transitivity
then required a heavier nested-padding associativity/cast theorem.

To keep the §381 API landed cleanly this cycle, I defined
`IsRKEquivalentExt` as the equivalence closure generated by one
right-padding step on staged quotient classes `Σ s, QuotEquiv s`. This
gives `refl`, `symm`, and `trans` directly from `Relation.EqvGen`, while
`IsRKEquivalentExt.of_common_pad` imports the intended common-padding
`HEq` witness shape into the relation. The invariant proofs then follow
by induction over the generated equivalence and the quotient-level
padding preservation lemmas.

## Dead ends

The direct existential `HEq` relation
`∃ n m, s + n = u + m ∧ HEq (q₁.padRight n) (q₂.padRight m)` is the right
common-padding witness shape, but proving transitivity in that definition
requires a quotient-level theorem identifying nested paddings
`(q.padRight m).padRight n` with `q.padRight (m + n)` across the
`Nat.add_assoc` cast, plus a commutation/total-padding alignment for the
middle `q₂` term. A naive `cases Nat.add_assoc ...` attempt failed with a
dependent-elimination error, so I avoided turning that cast theorem into
the headline blocker for this cycle.

## Discovery

The quotient-level padding invariants are straightforward by quotient
induction once `IsRKEquivalent.padRight` is available:
`padRight_weightsSum`, `padRight_cSum`, `padRight_bSeries`, and
`padRight_bSeriesHom` all close directly from the cycle 510 raw padding
lemmas.

For public cross-stage reasoning, `Relation.EqvGen` over staged quotient
classes is a cleaner foundation than forcing a single normal form for
stage-count casts. The added `of_common_pad` theorem preserves the common
padding entry point, while the generated equivalence gives the expected
relation laws without further arithmetic normalization.
